LIVE SKY OVER BEND

This is a live overhead star chart for central Bend, Oregon:
44.058 degrees north, 121.315 degrees west; elevation 1,100 metres.
Positions update each minute using the visitor's device clock, and refresh
when the tab becomes visible. Pause motion also freezes the sky's timestamp.
The displayed time is always Bend time (America/Los_Angeles, including DST).

One stereographic projection is shared by all stars, constellation lines,
the Moon and the ecliptic. Zenith is at the centre; north is up, east left,
south down and west right, as on a chart held overhead. The circular edge
is the geometric horizon. The map changes size, not orientation, on mobile.
Stars below the horizon are omitted; constellation segments with an endpoint
below the horizon are omitted rather than extended through the hidden sky.

MOON AND PATHS
The Moon is positioned topocentrically, including observer parallax and
light travel time. Its disc is shown only when its centre is above the
geometric horizon. The illuminated fraction is calculated from the Sun-Moon
geometry; the bright limb faces the Sun in this projection. The Moon's disc
is enlarged for legibility. The phase fraction uses a geocentric calculation.

The GOLD DASHED line is the Moon's apparent path over the NEXT 24 HOURS,
sampled every 10 minutes, including both lunar orbital motion and Earth's
rotation. It is not an ellipse in space or the Moon's orbit relative to the
fixed stars. Only the above-horizon portions of this future path are drawn.
The BLUE DOTTED line is the J2000 ecliptic: Earth's mean orbital plane.
Faint concentric rings mark altitude 0, 30 and 60 degrees.

This remains a dark star chart during daylight, with a daylight/twilight
label. It does not simulate local weather, terrain, atmospheric refraction,
light pollution or the actual visibility of stars. Twilight is Sun altitude
between 0 and -18 degrees. Twinkling and shooting stars are decorative
animations, not predictions of meteor events. Stars are rendered through
visual magnitude 5.5, plus fainter constellation anchors; sizes and colours
are stylized. This is an educational illustration, not a navigation tool.

CALCULATIONS
Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 by Don Cross, MIT license:
https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy
https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy/blob/master/LICENSE
J2000 coordinates are advanced by catalogue proper motion and transformed
to the observer's horizon with precession, nutation and Earth rotation.
Stellar annual aberration/parallax and radial-velocity perspective changes
are omitted at this illustration's scale.

STAR POSITIONS
Hoffleit, D. & Warren, W. H., Jr. (1991), The Bright Star Catalogue,
5th Revised Edition (preliminary version), Yale University Observatory.
CDS catalogue V/50: https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/V/50/ReadMe
2,887 stars, retrieved 2026-10-20. Source data are bundled with the site;
no external astronomy service or visitor geolocation is needed at runtime.
Rebuild with scripts/build-sky-catalog.py in the source repository.
